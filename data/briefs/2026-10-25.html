Cargo ship Khoryv sinks off Romania after drone strike, one dead

The Comoros-flagged general cargo ship sank about 13 nautical miles off Constanța on 23 October, with three crew missing.

Reporting period: the 24 hours to 04:38 UTC on 25 October 2026. By the iMariners Security Desk.

Key points

  • Romania's interior ministry said the drone strike caused major damage and a fire on the Khoryv.
  • WANA News Agency gave no name, flag or position for the tanker it reported hit in Hormuz.
  • The headline claim that U.S. forces struck three Iranian tankers carried no named source.
  • No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.

The general cargo ship Khoryv sank about 13 nautical miles off Constanța, Romania, on 23 October after a drone strike that killed one crew member, Romanian authorities said.

Merchant ships reported hit in the last seven days, according to the iMariners tracker: 1 in the Black Sea (5 the week before); 4 in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf (6 the week before).

Black Sea

Nine crew were rescued and the Romanian Naval Authority is leading the search for the three missing, Romania's interior ministry said. The ministry said the strike caused major damage and a fire. Romanian officials blamed Russia.

Earlier in the week, russia's defence ministry claimed its Geran drones struck dry cargo ships and a container ship in the Black Sea between 21 and 23 October, saying they carried military cargo for Ukraine, with one vessel reported adrift off Giresun. Separate statements from the same ministry said drones struck port infrastructure and a ship at Vilkovo port, without naming vessels or giving damage details.

Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf

WANA News Agency reported an explosion and possible damage to a giant oil tanker in the Strait of Hormuz on 24 October. The agency gave no vessel name, flag, position or attribution.

A headline said the U.S. struck three Iranian oil tankers after missile attacks on navy warships. No vessel names, positions, dates or damage details were given, and the claim was not attributed to a named source.

Earlier in the week, on 21 October, UKMTO reported that a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile about 11 nautical miles off Bukha, Oman, starting a fire that was extinguished with all crew safe; TradeWinds identified the vessel as the Kuwaiti product tanker Al Dasma.

Earlier in the week, on 21 October, UKMTO reported that a tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile in the northern Persian Gulf southeast of Iraq's Al Faw port, with all crew safe; Seatrade named the vessel as the Liberian-flagged tanker Soyia.

Earlier in the week, on 19 October, the IMO listed the tanker Chloe (Mivera V) as damaged with no pollution at 121 nautical miles from the coast of Oman.

Earlier in the week, on 22 October, UKMTO received a time-late report that a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile 7 nautical miles northeast of Limah, Oman, and was able to continue its voyage.

No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden in the last 24 hours.
