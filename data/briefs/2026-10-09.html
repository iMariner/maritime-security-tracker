Tanker Acers hit off Qatar with casualties, UKMTO says

The Antigua and Barbuda-flagged tanker was struck about 51 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal on 7 October, with casualties reported.

Reporting period: the 24 hours to 04:39 UTC on 9 October 2026. By the iMariners Security Desk.

Key points

  • The Antigua and Barbuda-flagged tanker Acers was hit by multiple projectiles off Qatar on 7 October, UKMTO said.
  • Vanguard, Splash247 and gCaptain named the vessel; the attacker has not been identified.
  • A crude oil tanker was struck in the Strait of Hormuz on 6 October, UKMTO said in a time-late warning issued on 8 October, with no casualties.
  • The UN paused its Hormuz escort after the 8 October attack on the Singapore-flagged Evergreen containership Ever Lovely near Oman, whose crew were safe.
  • Shots were fired at the Singapore-flagged containership CUL Klang in the southern Red Sea on 7 October, The Maritime Executive reported, with the crew unharmed and no claim of responsibility.

The Antigua and Barbuda-flagged tanker Acers was struck by multiple projectiles about 51 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar, at about 1900 UTC on 7 October, with casualties reported, UKMTO said.

It is one of six merchant ships reported hit in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf in the last seven days, the same number as in the seven days before, according to the iMariners tracker. The tracker also records one merchant ship hit in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden in the last seven days, against none in the seven days before, and four in the Black Sea, against none in the preceding week.

Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf

UKMTO confirmed the strike on the Acers, an Antigua and Barbuda-flagged tanker, with casualties reported. Vanguard, Splash247 and gCaptain named the vessel. The attacker has not been identified.

A crude oil tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 6 October, UKMTO said in a time-late warning issued on 8 October. No casualties were reported. The UN halted its escort of ships through Hormuz after the 8 October attack on the Singapore-flagged Evergreen containership Ever Lovely near Oman, whose crew were safe.

Earlier in the week, the Greek-flagged LNG carrier Maran Gas Mystras was confirmed hit by an unidentified projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 4 October, with its crew safe, Greece's Ministry of Shipping said.

Red Sea and Gulf of Aden

Shots were fired at the Singapore-flagged containership CUL Klang in the southern Red Sea on 7 October, The Maritime Executive reported. The crew were unharmed and no group has claimed responsibility.

Black Sea

TASS reported that Russian forces struck a cargo ship at sea that was delivering supplies to the Ukrainian port of Chornomorsk. The Russian Ministry of Defence claim gave no vessel name, flag, position or damage details.

Earlier in the week, the Marshall Islands-flagged tanker Aviva was hit in the Black Sea on 6 October, Turkey's Haber Denizde reported, with one seafarer killed and the fire put out. The Odesa administration reported the same day that a Marshall Islands vessel loaded with rapeseed oil was hit, with one killed and seven injured.

Earlier in the week, the Arina A was claimed hit by a drone off Trabzon on 5 October, with its captain killed, according to Gümüşkoza Gazetesi and Mersin Haber.

What crews should know

The iMariners tracker records six merchant ships reported hit in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf in the last seven days, the same as in the seven days before, and one in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden, against none in the preceding week. Four were reported hit in the Black Sea in the last seven days, against none in the seven days before.

Today's attacks took place in the Strait of Hormuz, the southern Red Sea and the Black Sea, including a strike about 51 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal.

Report any attack or suspicious approach to UKMTO, and follow UKMTO and JMIC advisories and company security instructions.

Corrections

Our 8 October brief reported that a fuel tanker was ablaze in the Strait of Hormuz after a drone strike, according to Iran's Revolutionary Guards; the EnergyNow item is a republished March 2026 report about the IRGC claim on the Honduran-flagged Athe Nova, not an attack on 7 October.

Our 8 October brief reported that an agricultural news outlet said ships carrying rapeseed oil and grain in the Black Sea were targeted; it names no ship, flag or position and repeats the 5 and 6 October Black Sea attacks already tracked.
