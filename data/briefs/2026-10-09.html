Tanker Acers hit off Qatar with casualties, UKMTO says

The Antigua and Barbuda-flagged tanker was struck by multiple projectiles about 51 nautical miles off Qatar on 7 October.

Reporting period: the 24 hours to 04:39 UTC on 9 October 2026. By the iMariners Security Desk.

Key points

  • UKMTO confirmed the Antigua and Barbuda-flagged tanker Acers was hit by multiple projectiles off Qatar on 7 October, with casualties reported.
  • A crude oil tanker was struck on 6 October in the Strait of Hormuz, UKMTO said in a time-late warning on 8 October, with no casualties.
  • The Singapore-flagged containership Ever Lovely was attacked near Oman on 8 October; its crew were safe, and a UN escort was paused.
  • A cargo ship bound for Chornomorsk was struck at sea on 8 October, Russia's defence ministry claimed via TASS, with no vessel details given.

The Antigua and Barbuda-flagged tanker Acers was struck by multiple projectiles about 51 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar, at about 1900 UTC on 7 October, with casualties reported, UKMTO said.

The strike is one of six merchant ships reported hit in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf in the last seven days, the same number as in the seven days before, according to the iMariners tracker.

Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf

UKMTO confirmed the Acers attack and said casualties were reported. The agency gave the position and time but did not name the attacker or the cause of the projectiles.

In a time-late warning on 8 October, UKMTO reported a crude oil tanker struck on 6 October in the Strait of Hormuz, with no casualties. The warning gave no vessel name or flag.

The Singapore-flagged containership Ever Lovely was attacked near Oman on 8 October, and its crew were safe, according to reports. A UN escort of ships through Hormuz was paused after that attack.

Black Sea

Russia's defence ministry claimed, via TASS, that its forces struck a general cargo ship at sea on 8 October as it delivered supplies to the Ukrainian port of Chornomorsk. The claim gave no vessel name, flag, position or damage details, and no independent confirmation was available.

Earlier in the week, Turkey's Haber Denizde reported that the Marshall Islands-flagged tanker Aviva, owned by Garanti Denizcilik and managed by Bilka Denizcilik, was hit in the Black Sea on 6 October, with one seafarer killed and the fire put out. The Odesa administration reported the same day that a Marshall Islands vessel loaded with rapeseed oil was hit, with one killed and seven injured.

Red Sea and Gulf of Aden

What crews should know

In the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf, six merchant ships were reported hit in the last seven days, the same as in the seven days before, according to the iMariners tracker. In the Black Sea, four were reported hit in the last seven days, against none in the seven days before.

Today's reported attacks took place in the Strait of Hormuz, near Oman and at sea bound for Chornomorsk. Report any attack or suspicious approach to UKMTO, and follow UKMTO and JMIC advisories and company security instructions.

Corrections

Our 8 October brief reported that a fuel tanker was ablaze in the Strait of Hormuz after a drone strike, according to Iran's Revolutionary Guards; the EnergyNow item is a republished March 2026 report about the IRGC claim on the Honduran-flagged Athe Nova, not an attack on 7 October.

Our 8 October brief reported that an agricultural news outlet said ships carrying rapeseed oil and grain in the Black Sea were targeted; the item names no ship, flag or position and repeats the 5 and 6 October Black Sea attacks already tracked.
