Crew missing after drone strike sets Wine Trader on fire off Romania

Five of the 12 crew were rescued from the San Marino-flagged chemical tanker after the 15 October attack near Sulina, Romanian authorities said.

Reporting period: the 24 hours to 04:38 UTC on 17 October 2026. By the iMariners Security Desk.

Key points

  • Romanian officials said a second tanker was also set on fire in the same area.
  • Bulgaria's president identified the sunken ship as HS Dignity; the Azov Concord took part in the rescue.
  • Five of the 15 crew were treated for light injuries after three drones hit Chelsea 3.
  • The IMO confirmed incidents list records one seafarer injured on DP World Kaveri.
  • No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.

Romania's president said drones struck the San Marino-flagged chemical tanker Wine Trader about 13 nautical miles southeast of Sulina on 15 October, setting it on fire with seven of its 12 crew missing.

Merchant ships reported hit in the last seven days, according to the iMariners tracker: 5 in the Black Sea (2 the week before); 9 in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf (9 the week before).

Black Sea

Five of the 12 crew were rescued and seven remain missing, Romanian authorities said. The position, about one mile outside Romanian territorial waters, was given by gCaptain and Al Jazeera. Romanian officials said a second tanker was also set on fire in the same area.

Bulgaria's prime minister said the Guinea-Bissau-flagged general cargo ship HS Dignity sank after a drone attack about 17 nautical miles east of Krapets on 13 October, with five crew rescued and 11 missing. Bulgaria's president named the vessel, and early reports naming the Azov Concord were wrong because that ship took part in the rescue.

Bulgaria's prime minister said the Maltese-flagged, Ukrainian-owned general cargo ship Chelsea 3 was hit by three drones about 18 nautical miles east of Byala early on 13 October. All 15 crew, seven Ukrainians and eight Azerbaijanis, were rescued and five were treated for light injuries, and the ship stayed afloat.

Earlier in the week, on 14 October, Romania's president confirmed drones struck the Comoros-flagged chemical tanker Yara J inside Romanian waters near Sulina, and her crew were unharmed.

Earlier in the week, on 15 October, Russia's defence ministry claimed its forces struck dry cargo ships carrying military and dual-use cargo, one in the Bug Estuary and one in the port of Chornomorsk. It gave no vessel names, flags or damage details.

Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf

The IMO confirmed incidents list records DP World Kaveri hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, with one seafarer injured.

The IMO confirmed incidents list records Ever Vict hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, with two seafarers injured.

Iran's navy reported a fire on an oil tanker in the Gulf of Oman on 16 October, giving no vessel name, flag, position or cause.

Earlier in the week, on 13 October, JMIC listed GFS Pride as struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz and sinking, and Singapore's Maritime and Port Authority confirmed the sinking with all 21 crew safely evacuated.

Earlier in the week, on 13 October, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded GFS Giselle hit 6 nautical miles off the coast of Oman, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, on 13 October, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded Al Saadiyat hit 1 nautical mile off the coast of Oman, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, on 11 October, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded Angola Prosperity hit 8 nautical miles off the coast of Oman, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, on 11 October, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded Al Maqam hit in the Strait of Hormuz, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, on 11 October, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded Kition M hit in the Strait of Hormuz, damaged with limited pollution reported.

No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden in the last 24 hours.
