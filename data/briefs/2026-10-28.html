Tanker attacked by drone in Black Sea transits Bosphorus

Turkish outlets reported the heavily damaged tanker passed through the Istanbul Strait, which was closed to traffic for its passage.

Reporting period: the 24 hours to 04:37 UTC on 28 October 2026. By the iMariners Security Desk.

Key points

  • Turkish outlets said the Istanbul Strait was closed to traffic for the damaged tanker's passage.
  • Zoom News reported blasts in Riyadh and an oil tanker on fire, without naming the vessel.
  • BurgasMedia said Ukrainian K-2 drones are now targeting Russian tankers in the Black Sea.
  • No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.

A tanker heavily damaged in a drone attack in the Black Sea transited the Bosphorus on 27 October, Turkish outlets reported, and the Istanbul Strait was closed to traffic for its passage.

Merchant ships reported hit in the last seven days, according to the iMariners tracker: 3 in the Black Sea (1 the week before); 2 in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf (6 the week before).

Black Sea

Turkish outlets reported the tanker's transit on 27 October but gave no vessel name, flag, date or position for the attack. The Istanbul Strait was closed to traffic for the passage. The extent of the damage and the condition of the crew were not reported.

BurgasMedia reported that Ukrainian K-2 drones are now hunting Russian tankers in the Black Sea. The outlet gave no specific vessel, date, position, damage or casualty details.

Earlier in the week, on 26 October, Russia's defence ministry claimed its drones struck dry cargo vessels bound for Odesa and Chornomorsk that it said carried military equipment for Ukraine, and also hit port infrastructure and warehouses at Chornomorsk.

Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf

Zoom News reported multiple blasts in Riyadh and an oil tanker catching fire on 28 October. The outlet gave no vessel name, flag, position, cause or casualty details.

Earlier in the week, on 26 October, a projectile strike disabled a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, and Lloyd's List reported the bulk carrier Cape Dao sank after the attack. UKMTO also reported a vessel on fire off Iraq's Al-Faw coast in the Persian Gulf the same day, with no name, cause or casualties given.

Earlier in the week, on 26 October, UKMTO issued a late warning that a vessel was on fire off Iraq's Al-Faw coast, giving no vessel name, flag, cause of the fire, damage details or casualties.

No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden in the last 24 hours.

What crews should know

Report any attack or suspicious approach to UKMTO, and follow UKMTO and JMIC advisories and company security instructions.
