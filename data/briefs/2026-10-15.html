Container ship GFS Pride sinks in Strait of Hormuz

The Singapore-flagged vessel went down on 14 October with three injured, UKMTO and maritime security sources said.

Reporting period: the 24 hours to 11:22 UTC on 15 October 2026. By the iMariners Security Desk.

Key points

  • UKMTO and maritime security sources reported the sinking; Iran's IRGC Navy claimed a containership hit a naval mine.

The Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride sank in the Strait of Hormuz on 14 October, with three injured, according to UKMTO and maritime security sources.

Merchant ships reported hit in the last seven days, according to the iMariners tracker: 12 in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf (9 the week before); 6 in the Black Sea (2 the week before).

Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf

UKMTO and maritime security sources reported the sinking of the Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride on 14 October. Iran's IRGC Navy claimed a containership sank after hitting a naval mine, without giving a vessel name, flag or position. It is not known what caused the sinking.

The IMO lists the DP World Kaveri as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, damaged with no pollution and one seafarer injured.

The IMO lists the Ever Vict as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, damaged with no pollution and two seafarers injured.

Earlier in the week, the IMO listed the GFS Giselle as hit 6NM from the coast of Oman on 13 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO listed the Al Saadiyat as hit 1 NM from the coast of Oman on 13 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO listed the Angola Prosperity as hit 8NM from the coast of Oman on 11 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO listed the Al Maqam as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO listed the Kition M as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, damaged with limited pollution reported.

Black Sea

Bulgaria's Prime Minister said the Guinea-Bissau-flagged general cargo ship HS Dignity sank after a drone attack about 17 nautical miles east of Krapets on 13 October. Bulgarian officials said five crew were rescued and 11 were missing, with the crew mainly Syrian and three Egyptians, according to the Interior Ministry. Bulgaria's President identified the vessel as the HS Dignity; early reports naming the Azov Concord were wrong, as that ship took part in the rescue.

The general cargo ship Wine Trader was reportedly hit by two drones in the Black Sea off Romania on 15 October, with seven sailors missing and the vessel on fire. Rescued sailors were brought to Constanța, according to B1TV, Libertatea and Observator News. The reports name no attacker.

The Comoros-flagged chemical tanker Yara J was anchored about 10 nautical miles southeast of Sulina on Wednesday evening when its crew reported an explosion and fire onboard, a suspected drone strike inside Romanian territorial waters. The vessel was in ballast and preparing to enter the Danube, and the fire was extinguished. Eight sailors are missing, according to Romania.

Bulgaria's Prime Minister said the Maltese-flagged, Ukrainian-owned general cargo ship Chelsea 3 was hit by three drones about 18 nautical miles east of Byala early on 13 October. All 15 crew, seven Ukrainians and eight Azerbaijanis, were rescued and five were treated for light injuries. The ship stayed afloat, listing and drifting without her crew.

Seven sailors are missing after a fire on a San Marino-flagged tanker off Romania's Black Sea coast on 15 October, Romanian authorities said. Reports linked the fire to fears of Russian drones. No vessel name or confirmed cause was given.

Russia's defence ministry claimed its forces struck two dry cargo ships on 15 October, one in the Bug Estuary and one in the port of Chornomorsk. It gave no vessel names, flags or damage details.

Russia's defence ministry claimed its forces struck a dry cargo ship at sea on 14 October that was delivering military and dual-use cargo to Odesa port. It gave no vessel name, flag or position.

Earlier in the week, Russia's defence ministry claimed its forces struck a dry cargo ship in the Bug Estuary on 13 October that was delivering military and dual-use cargo to Mykolaiv port, without giving a vessel name, flag, position, damage or casualty details.

No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden in the last 24 hours.
