Wine Trader on fire off Romania after drone strike, seven missing

The San Marino-flagged tanker was hit about 13 nautical miles southeast of Sulina on 15 October, Romania's president said.

Reporting period: the 24 hours to 04:38 UTC on 18 October 2026. By the iMariners Security Desk.

Key points

  • Romania's naval authority said five of the Wine Trader's 12 crew were rescued after the 15 October strike.
  • UKMTO said the tanker near Al Jubail was hit by an unknown projectile on 17 October and her crew were safe.
  • A headline reported an LNG carrier targeted off Yemen on 17 October, without naming the vessel.
  • A Romanian agricultural outlet reported a vessel sunk by drones near the Romanian border, naming no ship.

Romania's president said drones struck the San Marino-flagged tanker Wine Trader off Sulina on 15 October, setting her on fire with seven of 12 crew missing, Romanian authorities reported.

Merchant ships reported hit in the last seven days, according to the iMariners tracker: 5 in the Black Sea (2 the week before); 5 in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf (12 the week before); 1 in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden (1 the week before).

Black Sea

The tanker was about 13 nautical miles southeast of Sulina, one mile outside Romanian territorial waters, according to gCaptain and Al Jazeera. Romania's naval authority reported the fire and said five of the 12 crew were rescued. Romanian officials said a second tanker was also set on fire in the same area.

A Romanian agricultural news headline reported that a vessel was sunk by drones in the Black Sea near the border with Romania. The report gave no vessel name, flag, position or date, and no authority has confirmed it.

Two vessels previously damaged by drones ran aground off Istanbul in severe weather, Marine Insight reported. The report named neither vessel and gave no flag or date.

Russia's defence ministry claimed its drones struck a dry cargo ship carrying military and dual-use cargo about 40 km south of Odesa port on 17 October. The same claim said strikes hit Ukrainian steel plants and a military cargo ship, without naming any vessel or giving damage details.

A vessel attacked by drones in the Black Sea was towed away from the strike site, prospect.com.ru reported. The report gave no vessel name, flag, position or date.

Earlier in the week, on 14 October, the Comoros-flagged chemical tanker Yara J reported an explosion and fire while anchored about 10 nautical miles southeast of Sulina, and Romania's president confirmed drones struck her inside Romanian waters with all crew unharmed.

Earlier in the week, on 15 October, Russia's defence ministry claimed it struck dry cargo ships carrying military and dual-use cargo in the Bug estuary and at Chornomorsk, a claim Reuters said it could not verify.

Earlier in the week, on 15 October, a Turkish headline said attacks on vessels in the Black Sea would be discussed at NATO.

Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf

UKMTO reported that a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile near Al Jubail, Saudi Arabia, on 17 October, with a fire reported and the crew safe. Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy separately claimed a second tanker caught fire after hitting a naval mine.

India condemned an attack on a commercial vessel off Oman on 17 October in which 11 Indians were injured, according to INDIA New England News. The report named no vessel and gave no attack method.

A Turkish-language headline reported attacks on tankers near the Strait of Hormuz. It gave no vessel name, flag, position, damage or attribution.

Al Jazeera reported that the US and Iran both claim control of the Strait of Hormuz amid attacks on vessels, without naming any ship or giving damage details.

Times Now reported that Iran warned "Hormuz is closed" as multiple ships came under attack in the Strait of Hormuz, naming no vessel and giving no damage details.

Earlier in the week, on 13 October, the Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride sank in the Strait of Hormuz after being struck by an unknown projectile, and Singapore's Maritime and Port Authority confirmed all 21 crew were safely evacuated.

Earlier in the week, on 13 October, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded the Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Giselle hit 6 nautical miles off the coast of Oman, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, on 13 October, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded the Marshall Islands-flagged Al Saadiyat hit 1 nautical mile off the coast of Oman, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, on 16 October, an Indian shipping official said a crude oil tanker was targeted by a missile in the Strait of Hormuz.

Earlier in the week, on 16 October, Iran's navy reported a fire on an oil tanker in the Gulf of Oman, without naming the vessel or giving a cause.

Earlier in the week, on 15 October, a Ukrainian headline said Iran attacked tankers in the Strait of Hormuz while oil flows through the strait unexpectedly increased.

Red Sea and Gulf of Aden

A headline reported that an LNG carrier was targeted off the coast of Yemen on 17 October, with measures being taken to follow up on the incident. No vessel name, flag, position or damage details were given.
