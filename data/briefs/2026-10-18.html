Crew missing as fire hits tanker Wine Trader off Romania

Twelve crew were aboard the San Marino-flagged vessel struck by a drone near Sulina on 15 October, with seven still missing.

Reporting period: the 24 hours to 04:38 UTC on 18 October 2026. By the iMariners Security Desk.

Key points

  • Five of the Wine Trader's 12 crew were rescued after the 15 October drone strike, Romanian authorities said.
  • UKMTO said a skiff fired two shots at an LNG tanker south-west of Al Mukalla on 17 October, with no damage.
  • Russia's defence ministry claimed it struck a dry cargo ship south of Odesa on 17 October, giving no name.

Romania's president said a drone struck the San Marino-flagged tanker Wine Trader off Sulina on 15 October, setting her on fire, Romanian authorities said.

Merchant ships reported hit in the last seven days, according to the iMariners tracker: 5 in the Black Sea (2 the week before); 5 in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf (12 the week before); 1 in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden (1 the week before).

Black Sea

Romanian officials said the vessel was about 13 nautical miles southeast of Sulina, one mile outside Romanian territorial waters, according to gCaptain and Al Jazeera. Five of her 12 crew were rescued and seven remain missing. Romanian officials said a second tanker was also set on fire in the same area.

Russia's defence ministry claimed its drones struck a dry cargo ship carrying military and dual-use cargo about 40 km south of Odesa port on 17 October. The same claim said strikes hit Ukrainian steel plants and a military cargo ship. No vessel name, flag or damage details were given.

Earlier in the week, romania's president confirmed that drones struck the Comoros-flagged chemical tanker Yara J inside Romanian waters near Sulina late on 14 October, and her crew were unharmed. The nine Indian seafarers who reached Constanta were aboard the Yara J, according to the Indian embassy.

Earlier in the week, russia's defence ministry claimed its forces struck dry cargo ships carrying military and dual-use cargo, one in the Bug Estuary and one in the port of Chornomorsk, on 15 October. No vessel names, flags or damage details were given, and Reuters said it could not verify the claim.

Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf

Earlier in the week, jMIC lists the Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride as struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 13 October and sinking. Singapore's Maritime and Port Authority, the flag state, confirmed the sinking with all 21 crew safely evacuated.

Earlier in the week, the IMO confirmed incidents list records the Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Giselle hit 6 nautical miles off the coast of Oman on 13 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO confirmed incidents list records the Marshall Islands-flagged vessel Al Saadiyat hit 1 nautical mile off the coast of Oman on 13 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, uKMTO warning 174-26 records a tanker struck by an unknown projectile 68 nautical miles north-east of Al Jubail on 16 October, and warning 175-26 an RPG fired from a small boat at a tanker 78 nautical miles north-east the same day, both crews safe.

Earlier in the week, india's shipping minister Sarbananda Sonowal named the crude oil tanker MT ST 3, with 15 Indian seafarers aboard, struck by a projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 16 October, all safe.

Red Sea and Gulf of Aden

UKMTO warning 176-26 records a skiff with seven people firing two shots at an LNG tanker 140 nautical miles south-west of Al Mukalla on 17 October. Her armed security team fired warning shots and the skiff withdrew, with no damage and no injuries.

What crews should know

Report any attack or suspicious approach to UKMTO, and follow UKMTO and JMIC advisories and company security instructions.
