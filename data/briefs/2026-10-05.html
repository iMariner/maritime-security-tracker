Tanker hit in Strait of Hormuz, engine room damaged

UKMTO confirms a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 4 October, with further explosions reported off Yemen and in the Gulf of Aden.

Key points

  • A tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 4 October, damaging the engine room, UKMTO said.
  • A tanker reported multiple explosions and an attempted attack about 60 nautical miles south of Al-Mokha off Yemen on 4 October, UKMTO said.
  • A maritime authority reported explosions near a tanker in the Gulf of Aden on 4 October.
  • No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Black Sea in the last 24 hours.

A tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 4 October, damaging the engine room, according to UK maritime authorities.

UKMTO logged three separate incidents involving tankers on 4 October: one in the Strait of Hormuz, one off Yemen and one in the Gulf of Aden. No vessel names or flags were given in any of the three reports. The Black Sea was quiet.

Strait of Hormuz

UKMTO warning 150-26, dated 4 October 2026, reported that a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz. The engine room was damaged. The crew were reportedly safe.

A roundup by Seatrade Maritime reported at least four tankers attacked in Hormuz, including a Dynacom newbuild, with Iran blamed for the attacks. That attribution is a claim in the report, not a confirmed finding.

Red Sea

UKMTO warning 151-26, dated 4 October 2026, reported multiple explosions close to a tanker about 60 nautical miles south of Al-Mukha, near Bab el-Mandeb. UKMTO logged the event as suspicious activity. The crew were safe and there was no damage, UKMTO said.

The same report described an attempted attack near the vessel. No attacker details were given.

Gulf of Aden

A maritime authority reported explosions near a tanker in the Gulf of Aden on 4 October. No vessel name, flag, damage or attacker details were given.

Black Sea

No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Black Sea in the last 24 hours.

What this means for crews

Vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz, the Red Sea, the Gulf of Aden and the Black Sea should follow current UKMTO and JMIC guidance and their company security procedures. Report any suspicious approach or explosion to UKMTO without delay. Keep the bridge and engine room watch briefed on the latest warnings.

Corrections

  • AROYAT (Black Sea): an earlier report was checked and not confirmed. Mis-dated: the described attack is the 27 September drone strike on the Palau-flagged bulker AROYAT near Novorossiysk, not an incident on 3 October.
