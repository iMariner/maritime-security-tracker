Tanker hit in Strait of Hormuz, engine room damaged

Tanker struck by unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 4 October, engine room damaged, crew reported safe, UKMTO said. Vanguard Tech identified the vessel as LIPSI.

Key points

  • UKMTO said a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 4 October, damaging the engine room.
  • UKMTO said the crew were reported safe after the explosion.
  • UKMTO said a tanker reported multiple explosions about 60 nautical miles south of Al-Mokha, Yemen, on 4 October.
  • No vessel name, flag or damage details were given for the Red Sea tanker incident.
  • No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Black Sea in the last 24 hours.

UKMTO said a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 4 October, damaging the engine room.

The incident was one of two confirmed incidents reported to merchant shipping in the last 24 hours, the other in the Red Sea. No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Black Sea.

Strait of Hormuz

UKMTO warning 150-26, dated 4 October, reported that a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz. The engine room was damaged. UKMTO said the crew were reported safe.

Maritime security firm Vanguard Tech identified the vessel as the LIPSI, a new LR2 tanker linked to a Greek operator and a Chinese owner, according to media reports. The cause of the explosion has not been confirmed.

Red Sea and Gulf of Aden

UKMTO warning 151-26, dated 4 October, reported multiple explosions close to a tanker about 60 nautical miles south of Al-Mokha, Yemen, near Bab el-Mandeb. UKMTO logged the event as suspicious activity.

UKMTO said the crew were safe and there was no damage. No vessel name, flag or attacker details were given.

What this means for crews

Vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea should follow UKMTO and JMIC guidance, and report any suspicious approach or explosion to UKMTO without delay.

Ship managers should review company security procedures and confirm crew mustering and damage control drills are current before entering these areas.
