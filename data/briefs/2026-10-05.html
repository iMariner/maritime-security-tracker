Tanker LIPSI hit by projectile in Strait of Hormuz

A tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 4 October, damaging the engine room. UKMTO also reported explosions near a tanker off Yemen.

A tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 4 October, with damage to its engine room, according to the UK maritime agency UKMTO.

UKMTO also reported explosions near a tanker off Yemen on the same day. No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Black Sea in the last 24 hours.

Strait of Hormuz

UKMTO warning 150-26, dated 4 October, reported that a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz. The engine room was damaged, and the crew were reported safe.

Maritime security firm Vanguard Tech identified the vessel as LIPSI, a new LR2 tanker linked to a Greek operator and a Chinese owner. Attribution for the attack has not been confirmed.

Red Sea

UKMTO warning 151-26, dated 4 October, reported multiple explosions close to a tanker about 60 nautical miles south of Al-Mokha, Yemen, near Bab el-Mandeb. UKMTO logged the event as suspicious activity.

The crew were safe and no damage was reported. UKMTO did not give the vessel's name, flag or any details of the attacker.

Black Sea

No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Black Sea in the last 24 hours.

A previously reported attack on the Palau-flagged bulker AROYAT near Novorossiysk was mis-dated. The drone strike occurred on 27 September, not 3 October.

What this means for crews

Crews in the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea should follow UKMTO and JMIC guidance, and report any suspicious activity to UKMTO without delay. Ship managers should review company security procedures for these areas and keep crews informed of the latest warnings.

Corrections

  • AROYAT (Black Sea): an earlier report was checked and not confirmed. Mis-dated: the described attack is the 27 September drone strike on the Palau-flagged bulker AROYAT near Novorossiysk, not an incident on 3 October.
