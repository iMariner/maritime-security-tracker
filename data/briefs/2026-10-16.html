Container ship GFS Pride sinks in Strait of Hormuz, three injured

The Singapore-flagged container ship went down in the Strait of Hormuz on 14 October, with three crew injured, UKMTO reported.

Reporting period: the 24 hours to 04:38 UTC on 16 October 2026. By the iMariners Security Desk.

Key points

  • UKMTO reported the sinking of GFS Pride, a Singapore-flagged container ship, on 14 October.
  • Bulgaria's Prime Minister said HS Dignity sank after a drone attack 17 nautical miles east of Krapets.
  • Bulgaria's Prime Minister said Chelsea 3 was hit by three drones east of Byala, and all 15 crew were rescued.
  • Romania's President confirmed drones hit both tankers, including Wine Trader, where seven sailors are missing.
  • No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.

Merchant ships reported hit in the last seven days, according to the iMariners tracker: 11 in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf (8 the week before); 6 in the Black Sea (2 the week before).

Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf

UKMTO and maritime security sources reported the sinking of the Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride on 14 October. Iran's IRGC Navy claimed a containership sank after hitting a naval mine, without giving a vessel name, flag or position. The cause of the sinking has not been independently established.

The IMO lists DP WORLD KAVERI as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October. The ship was damaged with no pollution and one seafarer injured, according to the IMO confirmed incidents list.

The IMO lists EVER VICT as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October. The ship was damaged with no pollution and two seafarers injured, according to the IMO confirmed incidents list.

Earlier in the week, the IMO listed GFS GISELLE as hit 6NM from the coast of Oman on 13 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO listed AL SAADIYAT as hit 1 NM from the coast of Oman on 13 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO listed ANGOLA PROSPERITY as hit 8NM from the coast of Oman on 11 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO listed AL MAQAM as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO listed KITION M as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, damaged with limited pollution reported.

Black Sea

Bulgaria's Prime Minister said the Guinea-Bissau-flagged general cargo ship HS Dignity sank after a drone attack about 17 nautical miles east of Krapets on 13 October. Bulgarian officials said five crew were rescued and 11 were missing, with the crew mainly Syrian and three Egyptians, according to the Interior Ministry. Bulgaria's President identified the ship as HS Dignity; early reports naming the Azov Concord were wrong, as that ship took part in the rescue.

Bulgaria's Prime Minister said the Maltese-flagged, Ukrainian-owned general cargo ship Chelsea 3 was hit by three drones about 18 nautical miles east of Byala early on 13 October. All 15 crew, seven Ukrainians and eight Azerbaijanis, were rescued and five were treated for light injuries, and the ship stayed afloat.

The San Marino-flagged chemical tanker Wine Trader sent a distress signal on the morning of 15 October after a fire broke out about 13 nautical miles southeast of Sulina. Five of the 12 crew were rescued and seven remain missing, and Romanian President Nicusor Dan confirmed both tankers were hit by drones.

The Comoros-flagged chemical tanker Yara J was anchored about 10 nautical miles southeast of Sulina on Wednesday evening when its crew reported an explosion and fire onboard, a suspected drone strike inside Romanian territorial waters. The vessel was in ballast and preparing to enter the Danube, and the fire was extinguished.

Russia's defence ministry said its forces struck two dry cargo ships, one in the Bug Estuary and one in the port of Chornomorsk, on 15 October. No vessel names, flags or damage details were given.

A tanker from Turkiye caught fire off Romania on 15 October as attacks on ships in the Black Sea spread, according to Turkiye Today. No vessel name or cause was given.

A storm drove a war-damaged tanker ashore in Turkey on 15 October, Shipping Telegraph and The Maritime Executive reported. No vessel name, flag or position was given.

Earlier in the week, Greenpeace reported another huge oil slick in the Black Sea on 14 October following an attack on a tanker off Sochi's coastline, while Russia denies any pollution but has declared a high alert.

Earlier in the week, Russia's defence ministry claimed its forces struck a dry cargo ship at sea on 14 October that was delivering military and dual-use cargo to Odesa port, without giving a vessel name, flag or position.

Earlier in the week, Russia's defence ministry claimed its forces struck a dry cargo ship in the Bug Estuary on 13 October that was delivering military and dual-use cargo to Mykolaiv port, without giving a vessel name, flag, position, damage or casualty details.

No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden in the last 24 hours.
