Wine Trader ablaze off Romania, seven crew missing

The San Marino-flagged chemical tanker was struck by drones about 13 nautical miles southeast of Sulina on 15 October, with five of her 12 crew rescued.

Reporting period: the 24 hours to 04:38 UTC on 16 October 2026. By the iMariners Security Desk.

Key points

  • Five of the Wine Trader's 12 crew were rescued and seven remain missing after the drone strike.
  • Bulgaria's President identified the sunken ship as the Guinea-Bissau-flagged HS Dignity.
  • All 15 crew of the Chelsea 3 were rescued, with five treated for light injuries.
  • The IMO confirmed incidents list records one seafarer injured on the DP World Kaveri.
  • No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.

The San Marino-flagged chemical tanker Wine Trader was struck by drones about 13 nautical miles southeast of Sulina on 15 October, leaving seven of her 12 crew missing, Romanian authorities said.

Merchant ships reported hit in the last seven days, according to the iMariners tracker: 5 in the Black Sea (2 the week before); 11 in the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf (8 the week before).

Black Sea

The Wine Trader sent a distress signal on the morning of 15 October after a fire broke out, and five of her 12 crew were rescued while seven remain missing. Romanian President Nicusor Dan said both tankers were hit by drones. The search for the missing crew continued.

Bulgaria's Prime Minister said the Guinea-Bissau-flagged general cargo ship HS Dignity sank after a drone attack about 17 nautical miles east of Krapets on 13 October, with five crew rescued and 11 missing. Bulgaria's President identified the vessel; early reports naming the Azov Concord were wrong, as that ship took part in the rescue.

Bulgaria's Prime Minister said the Maltese-flagged, Ukrainian-owned general cargo ship Chelsea 3 was hit by three drones about 18 nautical miles east of Byala early on 13 October. All 15 crew, seven Ukrainians and eight Azerbaijanis, were rescued and five were treated for light injuries. The ship stayed afloat.

The Comoros-flagged chemical tanker Yara J was anchored about 10 nautical miles southeast of Sulina on the evening of 14 October when her crew reported an explosion and fire onboard, a suspected drone strike inside Romanian territorial waters. The crew extinguished the fire themselves and no one was hurt, and the vessel was in ballast preparing to enter the Danube.

Russia's defence ministry claimed its forces struck two dry cargo ships on 15 October, one in the Bug Estuary and one in the port of Chornomorsk. No vessel names, flags or damage details were given.

Earlier in the week, Russia's defence ministry claimed on 14 October that its forces struck a dry cargo ship at sea that was delivering military and dual-use cargo to Odesa port.

Earlier in the week, Russia's defence ministry claimed on 13 October that its forces struck a dry cargo ship in the Bug Estuary that was delivering military and dual-use cargo to Mykolaiv port.

Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf

The IMO confirmed incidents list records the DP World Kaveri as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, damaged with no pollution and one seafarer injured.

The IMO confirmed incidents list records the Ever Vict as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, damaged with no pollution and two seafarers injured.

Earlier in the week, Singapore's Maritime and Port Authority confirmed that the Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride sank after being struck by a projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on 14 October, with all 21 crew safely evacuated and no injuries.

Earlier in the week, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded the GFS Giselle as hit 6NM off the coast of Oman on 13 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded the Al Saadiyat as hit 1 NM off the coast of Oman on 13 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded the Angola Prosperity as hit 8NM off the coast of Oman on 11 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded the Al Maqam as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, damaged with no pollution.

Earlier in the week, the IMO confirmed incidents list recorded the Kition M as hit in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, damaged with limited pollution reported.

No new attacks on merchant ships were reported in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden in the last 24 hours.
